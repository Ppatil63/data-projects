# Insights — Logistics Delivery Performance

> **Data honesty note:** the 2,500 shipments in this project are *synthetic
> sample data* generated by `generate_data.py` (seeded, reproducible) to
> demonstrate the pipeline. The numbers below are really computed from that
> dataset by `etl.py` / `analysis.sql` — they describe the sample data, not
> a real carrier network.

- **Overall on-time delivery is 69.49%** — 700 of 2,294 delivered shipments
  arrived late, with an average delay of 2.03 days on the late ones.
- **You get what you pay for.** SwiftLine is the most expensive carrier at
  $2.88/kg and the most reliable: 85.4% on-time, arriving 0.17 days early
  on average. BlueRail Logistics is the cheapest at $1.81/kg — and delivers
  on time only 42.9% of the time with a 1.25-day average delay. The
  $1.07/kg "saving" buys a coin-flip delivery promise.
- **Late freight is a quarter of the spend.** The 700 late shipments cost
  $31,929.77 — 24.6% of the $129,545.42 total shipping spend — at $45.61
  per late shipment. Any SLA-penalty or re-delivery cost lands on top of
  that.
- **Carrier choice matters far more than geography.** Destination zones
  sit in a narrow band (Midwest best at 72.0% on-time, South Central worst
  at 67.2%), while carriers spread across 42.9%–85.4%. Fixing delivery
  performance starts with carrier allocation, not route planning.
- **Mid-pack is not safe either.** Apex Freight (68.0% on-time, $2.38/kg)
  and National Parcel Co (74.8%, $2.57/kg) both trail SwiftLine by 11–17
  points — shifting BlueRail's long-haul volume to either would lift the
  network average immediately.
- **No seasonal collapse, but no improvement either.** Monthly on-time
  rates oscillate between 65.4% (Sep) and 75.8% (Jul) across Apr–Sep 2026
  with no sustained trend — performance is structurally set by the carrier
  mix, not by the month.
